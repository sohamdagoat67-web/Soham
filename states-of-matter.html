📘 States of Matter

Matter can exist in different states depending on the arrangement and movement of its particles. The main states explored in this stage are solid, liquid, gas, plasma and Bose-Einstein condensate (B.E.C.).

In a solid, particles are tightly packed and mainly vibrate around fixed positions. Solids have a definite shape and volume.

In a liquid, particles remain relatively close together but can move past one another. A liquid has a definite volume but takes the shape of its container.

In a gas, particles are widely separated and move freely. A gas expands to fill the available space and has neither a fixed shape nor a fixed volume.

Plasma is a highly energetic, ionised state of matter. It contains charged particles and can conduct electricity.

A Bose-Einstein Condensate, or B.E.C., forms when certain particles are cooled to temperatures extremely close to absolute zero. The particles can behave collectively as a quantum system.

💡 Key Takeaways

  • 🧊 Solids have fixed shape and volume.
  • 💧 Liquids have fixed volume but no fixed shape.
  • 💨 Gases have neither fixed shape nor volume.
  • ⚡ Plasma is a highly energetic ionised state.
  • ⚛️ B.E.C. forms at extremely low temperatures.
